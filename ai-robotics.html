The AI and Robotics programme at London International School — Prof. Waris Mir Campus is an age-graded path that begins at Pre-Nursery (age 3) and runs through Class 7, with Class 8 launching in 2026-27. Children start with play-based digital literacy, move into block-based coding in Scratch, then earn two early coding certificates at Kindergarten through our US-based curriculum partner. There is no open chatbot in early years; all AI activity is teacher-led and age-appropriate.

Our philosophy

Why we start AI education at age three

The children beginning school in 2025 will retire in the 2080s. The most certain prediction we can make about their working lives is that they will be defined by decisions made alongside artificial intelligence: in medicine, engineering, design, law, and every field in between.

Teaching children to think with AI at age three is not about making them programmers. It is about making them comfortable with the technology that will shape their entire lives, so they grow up directing it instead of being directed by it.

This philosophy sits naturally alongside the inspiration behind our school, Prof. Waris Mir, whose belief that the purpose of education is "not to fill minds, but to open them" shapes everything we do. Our AI and robotics programme opens minds to what is possible, one developmentally appropriate stage at a time.

The ten-year path

From curious three-year-old to first invention

Each stage is developmentally matched to what children can do, want to do, and will need next. Nothing is rushed. Nothing is skipped.

3–4 yrs
Pre-Nursery and Nursery

Pre-Years: curious about computers

In their very first classroom, children are introduced to computers, AI and coding through play. The goal is not productivity. It is comfort: three and four year olds build confidence with technology, learn how it works, and start asking the curious questions that will carry them through a decade of study.

  • Digital literacy: how computers listen, look and respond
  • Play-based coding with colourful block-based tools
  • Teacher-led "how does this work?" sessions, age-appropriate
A parent told us

"My daughter comes home asking how the computer knows her name. Now she asks us better questions, too."

5 yrs
Unique in Pakistan
Kindergarten

Two early coding certificates, at five

This is the year that most clearly separates London International School from every other school in Pakistan. Our Kindergarten programme delivers two early coding qualifications aligned to the US K-12 Computer Science Framework, covering Scratch block-based programming and introductory Python concepts. Awarded in partnership with our US-based coding curriculum provider, and earned by every student who completes the year, not a select few.

  • First real code: Scratch for sequencing, Python basics
  • Two early coding certificates awarded at year-end through our US-based curriculum partner
  • Guaranteed for every student, not an optional elective
A Kindergarten parent

"My daughter leaves KG with two certifications from America. Her grandparents still can't quite believe it."

6–8 yrs
Classes 1–3

Early primary: first real robots

With the foundational thinking in place, children move from screen to physical world. Using LEGO Mindstorms kits, students design robots that actually move, sense, and react. The goal at this age is ownership: every child builds their own robot, names it, and demonstrates it to the class.

  • LEGO Mindstorms: design, build, and program moving machines
  • Circuit design and simple automation
  • Student-led classroom demonstrations by Class 3
A Class 2 parent

"He built a robot that sorts his LEGO by colour. I'm an engineer; I didn't do this until university."

9–11 yrs
Classes 4–6

Upper primary: real engineering

Now students move to the tools real engineers use. Arduino microcontrollers, ultrasonic and temperature sensors, motors and servos: the same components you would find in a university first-year engineering course. Projects shift to "build a thing that solves a problem at home", door alarms, plant-watering systems, light-tracking models.

  • Arduino boards with live sensors: industry-standard equipment
  • Circuit-to-problem engineering projects
  • Inter-class robotics competitions each term
A Class 5 parent

"My daughter isn't just using technology. She's the one deciding what it does."

12–14 yrs
Class 7 · Class 8 coming 2026–27

Lower secondary: invention-ready

By Class 7, our students have six years of hands-on technical experience behind them. This is where that experience compounds: they begin combining 3D-printed parts with Arduino circuits and AI-generated designs to create genuinely original prototypes. Class 8 launches in the 2026–27 academic year as our Class 7 cohort progresses, completing our Lower Secondary Cambridge pathway.

  • 3D printing integrated with AI-assisted design
  • National-level robotics and invention competitions
  • University-ready portfolios as Lower Secondary completes

Parent questions

About our AI & Robotics programme

At what age does AI and robotics education start at London International School?

AI-based learning begins at Pre-Nursery (age 3) with age-appropriate digital literacy and play-based coding on colourful block-based tools. Formal coding starts at Kindergarten (age 5), where every student earns two early coding certificates issued by our US-based curriculum partner. Physical robotics with LEGO Mindstorms begins in Class 1 (age 6).

Which coding certificates do students earn at Kindergarten?

Our programme delivers two American-certified early coding qualifications covering block-based programming (Scratch) and introductory Python concepts, assessed and certified by recognised US coding authorities. Every Kindergarten student who completes the year earns both.

Is AI learning safe for a three-year-old?

Yes. At Pre-Nursery and Nursery, AI-based learning is introduced through teacher-led activities, play-based coding with colourful digital blocks, and age-appropriate conversations about how technology works. There is no open chatbot, no personal data collected, and every activity is fully supervised by qualified early-years teachers.

What robotics equipment do you use?

Our purpose-built Robotics Lab includes LEGO Mindstorms kits (Classes 1–3), Arduino microcontrollers with live sensors (Classes 4–6), and 3D printers with AI prototyping tools (Class 7; Class 8 from 2026–27). Equipment is replaced each year to match the latest industry-standard hardware.

How is this different from an after-school coding bootcamp?

A bootcamp teaches a skill in a few weeks. Our programme is a ten-year integrated pathway: coding, robotics and AI concepts are woven into every subject (maths, science, even English) and mapped to the Cambridge curriculum. Students build cumulative mastery, not isolated skills.

Does every student take part, or is it optional?

Every student at London International School participates in the AI and robotics programme. It is a core part of the curriculum, not an elective. The two coding certificates in Kindergarten are guaranteed for every student who completes the year, not just a selected few.

What does the programme look like by Class 7?

By Class 7, students have built five years of hands-on technical experience across Scratch, LEGO Mindstorms, Arduino microcontrollers, 3D printing, and applied AI projects. They earn two early coding certificates at Kindergarten through our US-based curriculum partner and complete an age-graded portfolio of original prototypes. Class 8 launches in 2026-27 as the current Class 7 cohort progresses.
